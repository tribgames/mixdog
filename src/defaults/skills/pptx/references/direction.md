# Direction

Owns what the deck decides before full-deck composition: who reads it and how (§1), how it argues (§2), how it looks (§3), which direction the representative trial supports (§4), its colors (§5) and type (§6). `composition.md` owns the page, `kit.md` owns the code.

Rule strength: **Hard rule** (objective failure, with its check: `→ runtime \`code\`` when the review raises that code, `→ manual` when only the render shows it), **Default** (a sensible choice; override with a reason), **Reference** (vocabulary; no single right answer). An unlabeled line is a Default.

## 1. Reading mode — decides density before anything else
**Default — derive from delivery**: live projection and launches lean presentation; async review, approval, and leave-behinds lean text; hybrid review leans balanced.
| Mode | Carrier | Page grammar | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| presentation | presenter + visuals | one claim per slide, keywords, one large visual or hero number; explanation goes to notes | more, sparser slides |
| balanced | slide + presenter | one primary claim with a concise explanation, structured evidence, or a necessary list | mixed |
| text | the slide alone, read close | complete sentences, short paragraphs, captions, tables; bullets only for genuinely parallel items | fewer, fuller slides |

A presentation deck and a text deck from the same source differ in grammar, count, text volume, and rhythm — not only in type size.
**Reference — measured text volume** (ten frontier decks, September 2026, characters per page from the PDF text layer): analyst and IR pages carry 330-690 characters (Samsung 334, Naver 395, Kakao 610, NVIDIA 626, Bond 686 — a title, a chart or table with its labels, three to five commentary lines, a source) and a keynote page 140 (Sequoia: a title and one figure or one sentence). A balanced content page targets 250-500 Latin or 150-350 Korean characters across at least three carriers (title, evidence with labels, commentary, source); a text page more; a presentation page holds one claim and one figure and stays under 150. A page under the target with a strip-sized carrier is under-authored, not "clean".

## 2. Argument mode — decides slide order (one per deck, independent of style)
**Default — pyramid for anything that must land a recommendation; briefing for status and reference packs.**
| Mode | Skeleton | Slide-order tendency | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| pyramid | conclusion first, then structured support | cover → answer → 2-4 argument slides with evidence → implications → ask | decisions, analyses, board reports |
| narrative | situation → tension → resolution | cover → context → the problem sharpens → turning point → resolution → what changes | pitches, case studies |
| instructional | decompose, then step through | cover → map of parts → one slide per part → recap | training, explainers |
| showcase | presence leads, copy stays short | cover → hero visual/number → 3-5 reveal beats → closing image | launches, reveals |
| briefing | neutral, complete, scannable | cover → agenda → one topic per slide with even weight → summary | status updates, FAQs |

**Default — assertion titles**: a content slide's title is the finding, not the topic ("Retention, not acquisition, drives growth", never "Market overview"). Read in sequence, the titles are the argument.
**Hard rule — a user-supplied outline keeps its facts and relationships**; the mode may regroup and retitle unless the user presented the outline as the final plan. → runtime `number_without_fact` for figures; relationships → manual

## 3. Visual style — how it looks (no colors, no fonts)
A style is shape language, composition geometry, decoration density, whitespace, texture. It governs treatment and coherence; it never decides which carriers a slide may use, and it carries no HEX and no typeface (those come from §5 and §6). **The id is mechanical**: `deck({ style: '<row>' })` sets the page chrome, the corner radius, the rule stroke, the motif, the theme, and the accent policy from the row, so two decks of the same content open on different pages; the moves in the third column are what the author then composes on that frame.
**Default — one coherent visual language**: keep compatible type roles, color meanings, and treatments across the deck. The rows below are vocabulary, not exclusive categories; choose a different composition when the evidence calls for it, not a new decorative theme.

| Style | Shape language | Composition geometry (moves it reaches for) | Decoration | Texture / elevation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| swiss-minimal | grid-locked, sharp corners, hairlines | one oversized geometric plane zoning the page; asymmetric split flush to one axis; hero numeral at architectural scale; one diagonal rule as the grid break | near zero | flat |
| editorial | rectilinear scaffold, columns, thin rules | the title on the open canvas under a kicker, a rail at the left for the legend or a figure and the carrier on the main column; oversized numeral or drop cap anchoring the page; pull quote crossing columns; full-height vertical rule the content hangs from; figure crossing a column edge | typographic (kickers, hairlines) | flat; rules, bands, and air separate |
| photo-editorial | the picture is the page | full-bleed picture + directional scrim; caption registered to the picture edge; one line of large type on the calm zone | none beyond type | picture depth only |
| data-journalism | columns and sidebars cut into the grid | the native chart as the spine filling the body under the title, half the canvas; the legend in the rail; small-multiple strips; spanner rule dropping into a stat band; running source line at the foot; a dark head band (`chrome: 'bands'`) only for an IR-style deck | hairlines, source lines, rules | flat |
| soft-rounded | rounded fields, gentle radii | tinted rounded fields; simple connector diagrams; one lifted primary object; chevron runs | icons in tinted discs | gentle; one resting shadow |
| dark-tech | dark canvas, geometric precision | glow behind one metric; concentric rings around a central number; hexagon node clusters; ghost numeral behind content; thin bracket frames | glow, hairline brackets | luminous; no black shadows |
| glassmorphism | translucent panels on a radial field | hero panel off-axis over a bloom; overlapping discs; a glass ring around the key metric; panels stepped in depth | bright hairline edges | layered translucency |
| blueprint | schematic line work on dark paper | grid field with measurement ticks; dashed construction lines; leaders; isometric boxes from parallelograms | module tags | flat line work |
| brutalist | raw structure, heavy rules, irregular columns | masthead numeral crossing column rules; heavy full-bleed rule bars; one grid cell inverted to solid ink as the focal cell | none; weight is the decoration | flat, hard |
| custom | write the five columns yourself | — | — | — |

**Reference — choosing**: compare the subject's own edge, corner, opening, angle, or layering logic with each style's shape language before settling on rectangles and circles; a topic word never selects a row. Expressive print or hand-drawn looks (zine, memphis, chalkboard, pixel) need prepared artwork the runtime does not have.

## 4. Select from a representative visual trial
**Default — compare the open decisions on actual pages**: for a new direction, render two materially different compositions of the same representative content before building the whole deck (`review.md`). Each has a coherent reading mode, argument, palette, type, and treatment, but differs in how the message becomes visible. Choose by fidelity and reading clarity, or reject both; do not force a winner or rebuild every page twice. A supplied template, approved direction, or small factual edit skips the trial. Record the candidates and visible reason in the brief (`SKILL.md` §3); runtime candidate-count readings remain advisory.
**Default — anchors establish the subject**: lead with the actual object, a relevant artifact, a faithful visual explanation, or strong typography. A picture is useful when it identifies the subject or establishes a necessary setting, not merely because generation is available. Icons mark recognizable concepts, never fill space.
**Default — assets precede layout**: consult supplied brand material, existing product screenshots or artifacts, and a relevant reference deck when available. Record the selected asset's source, permission to reuse, and the detail it must reveal. Use ordinary tool permissions; a deck request does not authorize exposing private screen content or redistributing licensed template artwork. Do not invent a UI or performance result. A product pitch should show the product or its output where that demonstrates the benefit; a generic request-to-output diagram is not a substitute for accessible product evidence.
**Default — the cover establishes the subject**: choose an actual view, a relevant illustration, or strong typography for its communicative job, not media-lane availability. No generation lane still permits existing authorized assets. If useful assets are unavailable, record the limitation and use a faithful conceptual treatment without claiming it shows the real product. Read `pictures.md` only when a picture is selected.
**Default — the motif**: one device from the selected style repeats on anchor slides (cover, section, closing) and recurs on content slides only when it says something true (a real section numeral, a module tag, a deck word). A color bar or edge stripe alone is never a motif. → runtime `decorative_stripe` for stripes

## 5. Palette — from the subject, through one seed
**Hard rule — contrast**: body text ≥ 4.5:1; text ≥ 18 pt or bold ≥ 14 pt ≥ 3:1; white on an accent block ≥ 3:1. Accent is never body text. Meaning never rides on color alone. → runtime `low_contrast` (saved colors), `low_visual_contrast` (render); color-only meaning → manual
**Hard rule — colors are 6 hex digits without `#`**; alpha in the hex corrupts the file (`transparency: 0-100` on fills and images, `opacity: 0-1` on shadows). → runtime package validation fails at finalize
**Default — one hue family for the neutrals, the accent on its counter hue**: the kit's `palette({ hue })` derives the whole ladder from one seed hue — ink, body, muted, the three lines (lineSubtle, line, lineStrong — `composition.md` §6), paper, paperAlt, dark, darkAlt, onDark, onDarkMuted — with adjacent steps 10-25% apart in lightness and backgrounds within ±20° of the hue, and puts the accent (accent, accentDeep, tint, onDarkAccent) on `counterHue(hue)`: navy 225 → coral 15, indigo 250 → gold 40, forest 125 → amber 25, teal 185 → amber 30, berry 335 → gold 40, terracotta 8 → teal 198. The accent is the deck's second hue and its only saturated color on type and fields, on small areas; a state mark (below) is the one other saturated form. `accentHue: hue` keeps a single-hue deck (swiss-minimal, brutalist, blueprint, a brand that owns one color), and a brief may name any accent hue. Proportion starts at 60/30/10 (field / support / accent). The extremes are tinted, never 000000/FFFFFF.
**Default — four state colors, each with a word**: `T.state.positive | warning | critical | informative` say better / caution / worse / note — a verdict cell, a delta, a badge, a callout — and each comes in three forms: `solid` fills a mark (a dot, a delta arrow, a bar), `weak` is the field under a state word, `text` is the word on paper, paperAlt, or its own weak field (each ≥ 4.5:1; solid ≥ 3:1 on paper). The state hues are fixed (green 150, amber 40, red 5, blue 215), not derived from the seed, so a state means the same thing in every deck; a deck uses the states its content has, never all four for variety; a state's word or glyph always stands beside its color (the kit's `tone('positive')` pairs the field and the word for `badge()`, `callout()`, and `table({ verdict, tones })`).
**Hard rule — a state's solid form never sits under type**: type takes the `text` form (dark, restrained) on the `weak` field; the saturated form is the mark, so the deck's saturated hue on type stays the accent. → runtime `accent_hue_overuse` (saturated hue families on text-bearing shapes)
**Default — the accent has a type form and a mark form**: `T.accent` is the word (darkened to 4.5:1 on paper — a kicker, an emphasis run, a hero numeral); `T.accentFill` is the mark (S ≥ 0.78, L ≈ 0.5, ≥ 2:1 on paper — the reference marks sit at 1.3-2.6:1) that the one bar, disc, dot, or band takes; a mark that carries type (a badge, an active chevron, a numbered node) takes the `T.accentLabel` pair — the bright fill where white or ink reads on it at 4.5:1, else the type form under white. A bar painted with the type form reads as mud; a word set on a mid amber fails contrast. `T.accentFill` covers 3-20% of a content page and belongs to the object the title names; everything else it could have coloured is `T.mark` gray.
**Reference — measured colour** (ten frontier decks, September 2026, saturated pixels of every page): each deck has one bright accent that carries 53-98% of its saturated ink (Sequoia green 165°/S 1.0/L 0.3 98%, Evans red 0°/0.75/0.6 94%, Coatue blue 210°/0.75/0.5 93%, Naver green 135°/0.75/0.6 87%, Samsung navy 225°/0.5/0.2 83%, NVIDIA green 75°/0.75/0.4 67%, Kakao yellow 60°/1.0/0.6 39% + a pink 28%); at most two hues exceed 10% of it (Kakao four — a brand with a mascot). Saturated pixels cover 3-22% of a page (median 10%); dark pages are 0-33% of a deck (Samsung a third, NVIDIA 13%, Coatue 10%, most under 5%); pale tinted fields cover under 2% of a page.
**Default — saturation by area**: large fields S ≤ 20%, text S ≤ 25%, the accent S 60-90% and only on small areas; projected decks read 10-15% more saturated than the screen.
**Reference — temperature by subject**: tech/finance cool blues (hue 205-240), education/growth greens (100-160), health cyans (170-190), creative/marketing warm oranges and pinks (10-40), academic indigos (230-260). Seeds that have worked: navy 225 + ice accent 210; forest 125 + moss 90; coral 358 + navy 230; terracotta 8 + sage 150; teal 185 + mint 165; berry 335 + cream 40; slate 210 + cherry 350. If the colors would work on any other deck, choose again.

## 6. Typography
**Default — reliable families first**: Noto Sans/Serif (including KR, SC, TC, JP), Arial, Calibri, Cambria, Times New Roman, Courier New, Bookman Old Style, Century Schoolbook, Malgun Gothic, Microsoft YaHei, and Yu Gothic are practical starting points. Brand fonts are allowed when available to the recipient and renderer. If substitution changes metrics, use a compatible face or leave extra room and disclose the preview limitation; do not claim reliable fit from an approximate render. → runtime `unsafe_font_family`, `font_family_overuse` are compatibility diagnostics
**Hard rule — no `charSpacing` on Hangul or CJK**; Latin kickers only (the kit's `kicker()` decides by script). → runtime `cjk_letter_spacing`
**Default — Noto everywhere**: `typography({ script, pairing, fonts: 'noto' })` sets the display, sans, light, and data faces: Latin Noto Sans / Noto Serif with Arial for figures; Korean Noto Sans KR / Noto Serif KR; Japanese and Chinese their Noto families. `fonts: 'safe'` (Calibri/Cambria, Malgun Gothic) only when the user says the recipients lack Noto. Pairing: `serif` (serif display over sans body — editorial, data-journalism), `weight` (one family, bold display over regular body — most styles), `concord` (one family, one weight step — swiss, brutalist).
**Default — the scale starts from the reading mode**: `TYPE = typeScale(MODE)` provides body, lead, caption, kicker, section, title, cover, hero, stat (a band's numeral), and poster roles — body 13 / 15 / 20 pt for text / balanced / presentation, the title 1.8 × body (23 / 27 / 36), captions 0.75 × body (floor 10), kickers 0.65 × body (floor 9). Adapt and name the roles for this deck before reuse. The title orients; it need not be the largest object when a finding, image, or comparison is the point. Keep the fewest roles that establish a clear hierarchy at the intended reading distance.
**Reference — measured type** (ten frontier decks, September 2026, PDF text sizes normalised to a 540 pt canvas): analyst and IR content pages run body 10-14 pt (Naver 10, Kakao 10.5, Bond 11, NVIDIA 13.5, Coatue 14, Samsung 16) under a title of 16-30 pt (median 22; Kakao 17, Naver 13 as a small running head over a large chart); title / body is 1.0-2.1, never 2.5; the smallest type on the page is 7.5-12 pt; a keynote (Sequoia) reads body 20 under titles 30-66 with one 128 pt numeral. Hierarchy on those pages comes from weight (bold display over light body), one accent colour on the title's kicker or the key figure, and position (the title on its own band) — not from a title twice the body. Larger type on a fifth of the copy is what reads as a template.
**Default — coherent roles, adaptable scale**: reuse face, weight, size, color, and leading for recurring text roles. `TYPE`, `DIAG`, and `T` are convenient defaults, not the only allowed values. A display headline, brand specimen, or unusual composition may use a different size or color when it improves hierarchy and remains legible. Measure the actual choice; never shrink copy simply to keep an unsuitable layout. The receipt's `typeSet` and `textColors` help locate accidental drift, not enforce a count or palette quota.
**Default — three weights, not two**: prose in the light face (`T.light`), emphasis in the regular sans (`T.sans`), display and hero bold. Weight and size together separate roles; a role that differs by size alone from its neighbor is merged into it.
**Reference — measured mock type** (26 rendered style-gallery pages, 1280 × 720, September 2026, CSS sizes): the hero and editorial pages set the headline at 58-78 px (8-11% of the page height — 43-58 pt on this canvas, `TYPE.cover`), the deck paragraph at 15-17 px (1 × body), the KPI numerals at 32-58 px (`TYPE.stat`), the meta at 9-11 px; headline / body 4-5, the widest ratio of any corpus here and confined to the cover, the claim page, and the one thesis title (`display()`), never the content titles, which keep the analyst ratio above. Eleven of the 26 set one phrase of the headline apart (serif italic, or a noun in the accent — `emph:`).
**Default — leading is paired to the role**: each role carries its leading (kit `ROLES`) — poster, hero, stat, and kicker 1.0; cover 1.1; title and section 1.15; strong, caption, and label 1.2; lead, body, and note 1.35; prose 1.45 — one pair per role, so a caption's line pitch is the same on every slide. A numeric size without a role takes 1.2 at lead size and above, 1.35 under it. A box overrides `lh` for a stated job — a breathing statement up to 1.8, a numeral beside its unit at 1.0 — never to make copy fit. The paragraph step (`paraSpaceAfter` ≈ 0.6-0.8 × size) is visibly larger than the line step.
**Default — editorial wrapping**: measure the chosen text, then inspect its actual line endings. Keep Korean particles with their words and break display lines at phrase boundaries with `\n`; a box that fits can still read poorly. Shorten or rephrase, adjust the text measure, or choose a deliberate line break before shrinking type. Left-align prose unless another alignment carries the page's meaning; labels align to the objects they explain. Keep attribution subordinate and move implementation detail to notes when the audience does not need it on the page.
