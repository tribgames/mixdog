# Composition

Owns how one slide is composed once the direction exists (`direction.md`). This is vocabulary the author composes with, not a catalog the slide picks from: there is no slide type to select, only a page job, a relationship, and a composition move the author decides before writing a coordinate. `kit.md` draws the core page, `charts.md` adds native data carriers, and `pictures.md` adds the picture families.

Rule strength as in `direction.md`: **Hard rule** (with `→ runtime \`code\`` or `→ manual`), **Default**, **Reference**.

## 0. The page brief — decided before geometry (one plan line per slide)
Each slide's plan line in the brief (`SKILL.md` §3) names, in this order:
- **job** — cover · section · claim · evidence · process · comparison · structure · picture · closing (what the page does for the reader).
- **relationship** — the atom the content carries (§1), or `none` for a focal claim.
- **move** — what the reader now believes, decides, or does. **Hard rule — every slide states its move**: a slide that cannot state it is an outline defect — cut it or merge it, never decorate around it. → manual
- **composition** — the page-scale move (§2 lens + §3 recipe in ordinary words: "one dark field on the left third holding the number; evidence hangs from a vertical rule on the right").
- **carriers** — what carries the content: chart · table · picture · hero · statement · quote · specimen · diagram · prose · list (the runtime reads this back as information: `plan_promise_missing` is advisory).
- **texture** — prose · list · specimen · keywords (§8).
- **rhythm** — anchor · dense · breathing (§7).

The composition line explains how the claim becomes visible: who hands what to whom, which states differ, what a quantity compares against, or which detail in an artifact proves the point. Keep it inside the existing brief rather than adding another checklist. A paragraph can carry nuance; it should not repeat a relation the picture or diagram could communicate more directly.

## 1. Relationship atoms (Reference — vocabulary for the plan line)
| Atom | Meaning | Encode with | Topology axes to vary |
|---|---|---|---|
| order | sequence, progression, rank | position, numbering, direction, shared path | open or closed path; straight, bent, stepped, switchback, or coiled; level, rising, or falling; where the turns and the endpoint sit |
| link | dependency, exchange, influence | proximity when unmistakable, otherwise an edge | direct, hub, chain, split, merge, exchange, feedback; the fewest edges that carry the meaning |
| parent | one unit governs or decomposes into children | branching, indentation, nesting, scale | branch, indent, nest, radiate, scale |
| membership | units belong to a group, stage, lane | containment, shared field, band, repetition | enclose, band, lane, cluster, repeat, nest |
| contrast | peers, states, options compare | shared baseline, opposing regions, parallel framing | semantic axes, opposing or parallel fields, counterweight, before/after boundary |
| overlap | units share a subset or duty | intersecting regions with a legible common area | paired, chained, layered intersections |
| focal claim | one statement or number | scale and air | statement alone, hero number, pull quote, receded picture behind oversized type |
| evidence | a chart, table, or picture proves the claim | the evidence as the field | chart as spine with a takeaway, table with a verdict column, picture with annotation |

**Hard rule — counts never imply form**: a count of items never implies equal size, spacing, angle, or symmetry; closure, mirroring, centrality, and interlock require a meaning already in the content. → manual
**Default — meaning chooses topology**: repeated relationships may reuse the same form for comparison. Change the form when the meaning changes, not to satisfy a variety quota. Do not invent a causal arrow, ranking, or hierarchy the source does not support.

**Default — evidence carries the finding**: place the relevant artifact, native chart, or faithful diagram at the scale needed to read it. A chart's key comparison gets direct labels or a concise annotation at the relevant mark; definitions, units, and reference points remain available. Put the visual explanation on the page and qualifications in a short caption or notes, rather than attaching a second essay beside it. Illustrative schematics are labeled as such and never imply measured values.

## 2. Composition lenses — the page-scale move
Before coordinates, name the move the page makes and the jobs it serves (surface, boundary, focal mark, shared region, counterweight, direction). Then compose from these lenses; none is a checklist, each is a way a page can be organized.
| Lens | Use when it strengthens the page |
|---|---|
| Page field | one large surface, outline, aperture, or off-canvas contour organizes the major zones instead of a card per unit — a dark third, a tinted band, a plane cut on a diagonal |
| Outline carrier | a frame, arc, bracket, or band with no fill and a coherent stroke gives bare text ownership without a heavy card |
| Nested fields | an inset contour, secondary surface, badge, or focal shape inside or across a larger field creates hierarchy; siblings, never cards inside cards |
| Continuity | independent shapes aligned or overlapped across zones reinforce the reading path; a rule the content hangs from; a figure crossing a column edge |
| Depth and contrast | filled, outlined, offset, and negative-space atoms combine; one lifted object above flat peers; one light source |
| Deck language | a corner, arc, slant, notch, or layering logic recurs across the deck with page-fit variation, never a cloned composition |

**Default — structure before containers**: decide the relationship and reading path before choosing fields, cards, or columns. Containers group real units; neither a card nor a large plane makes unrelated text into a visual explanation.
**Default — grouping can be invisible**: alignment, proximity, and scale can bind text without a visible box or rule. Add a carrier only when it clarifies ownership or the reading path.

## 3. Page recipes — back to front, omit every layer without a job (Reference)
| Page job | Back-to-front stack | Stop when |
|---|---|---|
| cover | hero field — a full accent or dark field (Naver, Sequoia), a full-bleed picture under a scrim (Samsung, NVIDIA), or paper with a wordmark and one illustration field (Kakao) → optional scrim or wash → one purposeful contour or motif at full size (`motif()`) → kicker + title + one line (date · author), never a list. Without a picture the title itself is the anchor: `poster()` sets the claim at poster scale on the field (Sequoia, a16z), and the one hero object stands on the right third — `orb()`, the lit sphere half the page tall that 13 of 26 measured mock covers carry (the object 0.26-0.67 of the canvas; a motif at low alpha reads as background and a beat without an object measures 0) | copy is safe and title and field read as one object |
| section | the cover's field again with the section name (Sequoia), a progressive agenda on the dark field with the current section lit (`agenda()`, the Coatue device), the section's number at 220 pt beside its claim (`numeralBeat()`, its default `size`; the a16z opener — every section a different accent field), or the label alone on paper (Kakao), or the section on its own picture — `picture()` full-bleed under a `scrim()`, the number and name in the scrim's light (the photo section of the Sequoia and Coatue decks; the one section device that carries a picture, so a photo deck's marks are not its cover repeated) → restrained wash → the motif at half size. Three devices, one deck: the section marks vary between them, never the same beat repeated | the deck language is recognizable and the reader knows where the deck is |
| claim | quiet field → the statement or number at scale → one emphasis run or attribution → air where it gives the focal element room (below or beside, entering high). On paper the editorial hero (the frontier text page, measured across 26 rendered mock pages, September 2026): kicker → `display()` at cover size on two lines over 45-60% of the width → the deck line at lead size → a `statBand()` of three or four peers, or the one hero object (a picture, a product, a disc) on the right third → `dateline()` and the source closing the corners. Such a page measures 0.02-0.09 largest object like ours; the headline at 4-5 × the body, the strip under it, and the four corners are what separate it — never a card under the text | 40-60% of the canvas is deliberately empty |
| evidence | title (+ sub line) on the open canvas, the source line at the foot → native chart/table/leaders filling the body between them, 40-60% of the canvas → the legend, a hero figure, or the kicker in the rail → optional takeaway on the dark field | the claim is native and the evidence is the largest element |
| process | context field → native relation lines (spine first) → nodes and labels → one state or direction focus | every connector is semantic and ends on a node edge |
| comparison | matched planes seamed by weight → optional shared wash or divider → matched labels → one difference marker on one side only | crop, elevation, and paint stay symmetric unless asymmetry is the claim |
| structure | one field or outline → hierarchy by nesting, indentation, or scale → labels registered to their unit | placement alone communicates with color stripped |
| picture | the picture family from `pictures.md` §2 — on a body page almost always the artifact row (`tiles()`: three or four screenshots, stills, or clippings on one baseline, a label and a caption each) or the collage (`clippings()`); the full-bleed photograph under a scrim is a cover or section device → modifier → native type on the calm zone | the captions say what each artifact proves; text sits on a scrim or beside the picture, never on it bare |
| closing | the cover's field echoed (every reference deck closes on its cover's field: Samsung's photo, Naver's green, NVIDIA's swoosh, Kakao's mascot) → echoed contour or gradient, the motif at a corner, the cover's hero object at half size (`orb()`) → the ask or the thanks as the one line, in the on-dark accent (`poster()` at a step under the cover's size) → contact/meta | the ask is the most legible line |

More peers than a structure holds is two slides or a different atom — six options are a table, not six cards. Content over the reading mode's budget (`TYPE.body` 15 pt in balanced mode, 20 pt in presentation, 13 pt in text — the same box holds about a third less in presentation mode and a third more in text mode, by line count and measure together) is split or cut, never squeezed with a smaller size than the scale gives.

**Default — measure down before drawing**: the body top comes back from `head()`; `avail(top)` is the height left above the foot, and the blocks are chosen to fit it, not drawn and then found over the source line. A page with a carrier and a row under it shares the height out first (`const top = head(…); const room = avail(top); const carrierH = room − rowH − GAP.between`), a rail takes as many readings as `avail(top)` holds at body size — `readingH(w, blocks)` says how much; measured on the seam's narrow side (4.44 in), a labelled one-line reading is 0.71 in in text mode, 0.77 in balanced, 0.93 in presentation, and each further line adds 0.25 / 0.29 / 0.39 in, so the rail holds three two-line readings in text or balanced mode (two under a sub line) and two in presentation mode (one under a sub line) — a table takes `Math.floor((avail(top) − rowH) / rowH)` rows. `reading()`, `columns()`, `tiles()`, `statBand()`, `cards()`, `table()`, `ruledList()`, `braceGroups()`, `iconRow()` and `specimen()` refuse a block that would cross the foot and say by how much; the answer is fewer or shorter blocks, or a higher start — never a smaller size than the scale gives. The structure helpers refuse under their floors the same way, before the draw and with the fix named: `stage()` and `quadrants()` under 0.22 of the canvas, `lanes()` under 0.8 in a lane, `steps()` under 0.7 in a block (a detail needs 1.0 in), `dumbbell()` under 0.5 in a row, `loop()` under a 0.6 in radius, `hub()` on a stage whose inner radius is under 1.6 in — the answer is `avail(top)`, `shareDown()`, or fewer units, never a helper drawn small.

## 5. Construction order and registration
**Default — spine → nodes → connectors → labels → garnish**: the spine fixes entry and direction before any node is placed; nodes get one home each; only unresolved semantic links become connectors; labels attach visibly to what they explain; garnish is whatever can be removed without losing meaning.
**Hard rule — registration**: connectors end on node boundaries, chevron tips enter the next notch, seams meet across the full width, nested shapes keep a visible margin, one light source per slide. → runtime `shape_overlap` for text; connectors and seams → manual

## 6. Weight, spacing, grid
**Hard rule — edges and gaps**: 0.5 in minimum from every slide edge (page number and source line may sit at 0.4 in; a deliberate bleed — a picture, a band — is exempt); blocks at least 0.3 in apart, except a numeral directly above its own label and a kicker above its title (`GAP.bind` / `GAP.within`). → runtime `edge_margin`, `shape_out_of_bounds`, `text_spacing_tight`, `shape_overlap`
**Default — spacing expresses grouping**: keep a label closer to its object than to an unrelated block. `GAP.within`, `GAP.between`, and `GAP.bind` are starting values, not mandatory distances.
**Default — consistent relationships, flexible geometry**: reuse margins and peer spacing where the content repeats. The kit's `SPACE`, `GUTTER`, and `PAD` reduce accidental drift, but optical alignment and brand grids may use measured or custom distances. Judge grouping and balance in the rendered page; a short `gapSet` or matching right edges is not a design goal by itself.
**Default — three line strengths, three surfaces**: a line says what it separates — `T.lineSubtle` between repeated items (table rows, list rows, the peers of a stat band), `T.line` at a section boundary (a rule under a band, a divider between blocks; `hairline()`'s default), `T.lineStrong` as the outline that owns a region (`outline()`, a callout's edge, a frame). A surface says what level it sits on — `T.paperAlt` is the basement a module rests in (`field()`, a table header), `T.paper` the default canvas and a card on the field, `lift()` the one floating object (§9 depth). The table helper draws its row rules subtle and its header on the basement; a stronger rule between rows separates sections, and the page says so.
**Default — proportions serve comparison and emphasis**: use equal regions when shared scale helps comparison, and unequal regions when the evidence or reading path needs them. `spans()`, `splitAt()`, and `flow()` calculate chosen regions; text length alone does not decide their importance.
**Default — shared grid, flexible composition**: reuse margins, spacing relationships, type roles, and source treatment. `zones()` and `head()` suit pages that genuinely share a header and body; they are optional, not a universal skeleton. The title position, body start, and split may change to fit a dominant image, diagram, or comparison. Align repeated components; do not force unlike pages onto the same seam.
**Default — the carrier has presence**: on an evidence, process, comparison, structure, or picture page the primary carrier is the largest object and takes 40-60% of the canvas (receipt `observe.presence`, `deck.rhythm.presence`; a quarter is the floor, not the target): the chart's frame spans the body column from the head band to the foot band, a diagram's nodes are page objects (a 2.5 in disc, a 1.3 in block), a table's rows run 2.2 × the body size (0.46 in at 15 pt — the IR tables measured in Kakao and Samsung run 1.9-2.1 × theirs) and the table takes the body column, ten to fifteen rows, not three. Enlarge the chart, table, diagram, or picture first and give the copy the column that remains, never the reverse; a chart 8 in wide and 3 in tall beside a hero number reads as a strip. A page whose largest object is a paragraph is a claim or text page, and its plan line says so (`carriers: statement` or `prose`). Frontier decks read at thumbnail scale because one object per page is large; ours read small when the carrier is a strip beside three paragraphs. A structure (a loop, a hub, a dumbbell row, a timeline) does not reach that share on the open paper — its nodes and lines cover a tenth of the page however large the radius — so it stands on a `stage()`: the context field that fills its column from the body top to the foot (the bento card, Coatue's pale band), the structure sized from the stage's geometry, the rail beside it. The stage is then the page's largest object and the structure reads as the carrier. When the reading sits under the stage instead of beside it (a timeline, a step run, or lanes over two or three columns), measure the row first and give the stage the rest — `shareDown(top, columnsH(...))` — never a typed stage height: the guessed 2.2-2.5 in stages measured 0.20-0.22 of the canvas with a hollow band under the spine, the measured ones 0.22-0.30 with the spine centred (`timeline({ h })`), and a row that would push the stage under the quarter floor is refused before the draw with the row named.
**Default — judge visible weight**: inspect the primary object and its supporting groups together. Enlarge an unreadable visual or compress weak supporting copy before stretching gaps or adding a plane to fill space. Empty space is useful when it strengthens focus; a field promising more content than it holds is not. A sparse page can be balanced off-center, and a dense page need not reach a prescribed bottom coordinate. Use `stack()` for an intentional vertical distribution, not to manufacture fullness.
**Default — edit the page, not only its coordinates**: a large assertion title does not earn a large title area. Shorten it and let the primary evidence dominate when that is the page's job. Keep summary, analytical comparison, detailed reference, and caveats at their appropriate reading densities; move a dense table to its own page rather than reducing it to a strip below a chart. Display only the precision the comparison needs, disclose rounding, and retain exact source values in native data or a readable reference table. Reuse color meanings across pages; prior/current years must not swap emphasis merely because series order changed.
**Default — proportions follow information weight**: on a breathing slide the empty area sits where it gives the focal element room to be read, not as equal bands above and below a centered strip; on a dense slide the stat or stage the slide is about is the widest, and a comparison's changed side gets the larger plane.

## 7. Rhythm across the deck
**Hard rule — the deck is not one page repeated**: the same coarse grammar (title, a plane on the same third, a text column in the same place) on most content pages, or the same composition and carrier three pages in a row, is measured from the shapes. A relationship that changes takes a different structure (`kit.md` §6); pages that say the same thing merge. A deliberate series (small multiples across pages, matched comparison pages) declares it: `design: { review: { allowRepetition: true } }` on qa and finalize. → runtime `repeated_layout_grammar`, `consecutive_composition_repeat`
**Default — pace follows the argument**: a finding may need a dense evidence page and its implication a quiet statement. At contact-sheet scale, check whether each page has a clear focal object and whether transitions help the reader. Keep supporting reference pages before the closing statement, or explicitly label a post-closing appendix; an unlabeled data page after the ending reopens the story. Consistent comparison pages may repeat; do not alternate backgrounds or rearrange diagrams merely to make a pattern.
**Reference — measured frontier pages** (pixel reads of ten decks, September 2026 — Bond, Evans, BCG, YC, NVIDIA, Samsung, Kakao, Naver, Sequoia, Coatue, 280 pages; our decks beside them): background share runs 0.33 (BCG) to 0.83 (Sequoia), median 0.65 — air alone is not the fault; the largest connected object is 0.21-0.71 of the canvas (median 0.4) against our 0.21 before this work; uniform colour fields 0.03-0.31; saturated ink 3-22% of the page. Their titles are not larger (16-30 pt on a 540 pt canvas, `direction.md` §6), and nine of ten carry the title on the open paper with no band. What separates the decks is pacing, not any one page: across a pitch, keynote, or consulting deck the colourfulness of consecutive pages swings widely (standard deviation across pages 16-36 — Sequoia 36, NVIDIA 25, Kakao 18, Coatue 18, Evans 17, BCG 16: a text page, then a page with one bright chart, then a dark or picture beat), while a one-template IR deck sits at 5-8 (Bond, Samsung) and reads as a template however clean each page is. SlidesGen-Bench (EMNLP 2026), calibrated on 1.5k human preference pairs, puts the preferred deck-level colourfulness spread near 15 ± 6; our decks measured 5-12. `T.accentFill` on the one bar that matters, `Z.rail` / `Z.main`, body-size structure labels, the dark takeaway band, and the `rhythm:` column of the plan line come from these reads.
**Default — pace by colour, not only by density**: in a deck of eight or more pages, plan the sequence so a quiet page (text, table — colourfulness under 10) is followed within two pages by a page that carries colour (a chart with its accent bar, a picture, a dark beat, an accent field), and never three dark or three picture pages in a row; a deck in which every page carries the same amount of colour reads as one template whatever its layouts. Colourfulness is area-weighted: a tinted mark (a loop's quiet segments in `T.tint`, an accent word) does not move a page (C5 measured 9.6 before and after), only a coloured area does — a field, a chart's bars, a picture, a dark beat. The receipt reads this after the render: `deck.rhythm.colour` is each page's colourfulness and `deck.pacing` its spread (`colourSpread`, the frontier 16-36), the longest run of quiet pages (`longestQuiet`), and the plan lines that promised `rhythm: anchor` on a page that rendered quiet (`quietAnchors`); the plan line's `rhythm:` column decides it before.
**Reference — the deck's shape** (the same thirteen decks plus Krafton, LG, Spotify, and a16z — 400 pages classified by pixel grammar, September 2026): pages that are a beat (a dark or coloured field with a statement) run 0-21% of a deck (median 13% — BCG, Bond, and LG none; Sequoia 21%; Samsung 33% of a 15-page deck is four front-matter pages; Krafton's 38% is a deck dark throughout, a theme); pages carrying evidence (a chart, a table, a picture, a structure) run 50-97% (median 67% — LG 87%, Kakao 85%, Bond 97%; even Sequoia's memo-style deck puts a chart or a table on one page in five); text-only pages 2-30% (median 13%) and these are multi-column in Sequoia (67% of its pages run two to five columns), Naver (43%), Kakao (37%); no reference deck puts three beats in a row past its front matter. Our decks before this work ran beats on 26-30% of their pages, evidence on 39-40%, and one had three dark beats in a row. → runtime `beat_share_high` (advisory, the pages between cover and closing), `consecutive_beats`; receipt `deck.shape`, `deck.rhythm.grammar`
**Reference — measured mock pages** (26 rendered pages of an open-source styles gallery — Linear, Stripe, Apple, NYT, Anthropic, Bauhaus, editorial idioms — at 1280 × 720, September 2026, the same pixel reads): background share 0.03-0.98 (median 0.63), largest object 0.02-0.97 (median 0.36), ink box 0.79-1.0 (median 0.94), a dark field on 35%. The pages split in two: half carry one hero object on the right third (a sphere, a product, a photograph, a chart card — largest 0.26-0.97) and half are editorial text pages (largest 0.02-0.09, air 0.81-0.92) that still read as frontier. On the text pages the display headline is 8-11% of the page height (4-5 × the 2.1-2.4% body, the meta at 1.3-1.5%), a strip of numerals or short labels sits under the deck paragraph, and meta closes all four corners (ink box 0.8-1.0 against 0.68-0.8 on our text pages). Our text pages match them on air and largest object and lose on the headline's scale (`tallRunShare` 0-0.09 against 0.04-0.35) and the corners. → kit `display()`, `dateline()`, `tableRows()`; receipt `observe.presence`, `page_underfill`
**Reference — the deck's sequence** (the same 400 pages read in order, September 2026): between two marks (cover, section field, closing) the reference decks run 5-13 evidence pages (a16z 7·4·6·7·8·8, Spotify 9·7·10, Coatue 12·6·7, Sequoia 4·7·5·13·7; NVIDIA 6·5 in 15 pages); the marks that come closer than that are front matter (Samsung's four opening pages, Krafton's chapter openers at 0.08-0.29) or the closing pair. Consecutive pages change their page grammar (chart → text → picture → structure) on 0.35-0.65 of the page turns (median 0.46; NVIDIA 0.79); Samsung at 0.14 — every body page the same picture band — is the one template-like deck of the thirteen, and LG at 0.23 is a run of chart pages. Text-only pages come one or two at a time (BCG, Bond, Kakao, Krafton, NVIDIA, a16z, LG ≤ 2; Evans 3; Spotify 4); the longer runs are one deck's idiom (Sequoia's typographic memo pages, Coatue's 7, Naver's 5).
**Default — mark the deck at the run length the reference keeps**: in a deck of 8-12 pages the marks are the cover, at most one section field, and the closing (our A/B/C: 2-3 of 8; the demo 3 of 11); in a longer deck a section field every 5-13 pages, never two marks within three pages past the front matter. Between the marks let the page grammar turn on about every other page — a chart page followed by the reading page or the picture page that argues from it — and never the same grammar on five consecutive body pages unless the deck is a theme (a dark deck, an IR band deck) and says so in its brief; never more than three text-only pages in a row. The receipt reads the sequence back after the render: `deck.rhythm.grammar` (the turnover), `deck.shape` (the shares), `consecutive_beats`.
**Default — a dark deck is a theme, not a run of beats**: `deck({ theme: 'dark' })` (Krafton: charcoal body pages, white type, light-grey marks, one bright accent) swaps the tokens once; its body pages carry their evidence like any other and the receipt reads them as evidence, the beats a step darker. Never a dark page here and there in a light deck — that is what `consecutive_beats` and `beat_share_high` measure.
**Default — the deck's shape follows the argument, and the argument is mostly evidence**: cover, one section mark per chapter, closing — the beats — and every other page carries its evidence at half the canvas. A ten-page deck has two or three beats, not five; a claim page (`job: claim`) is a body page on paper with a hero number or a statement and its reading, not another field. Plan the sequence in the brief's `rhythm:` line and check it in the receipt's `deck.shape` before the render.
**Reference — receipt as a diagnostic**: `air`, `contentAir`, `fieldFill`, `renderAir`, `centroid`, `bodyFill`, and the type/spacing sets describe the artifact. They can locate a suspicious empty region or inconsistency, but cannot decide relevance, beauty, or intentional emphasis. Inspect the page before changing it; no occupancy ratio, balanced centroid, or short token set proves design quality.

## 8. Text on the page
**Default — a body page carries its reading, not one sentence**: the reference body pages carry 330-900 characters (Krafton 540, LG 900, Spotify 720 at the median; ours ran 60-150 before this work) — the title, the chart's own labels, and two to four short readings beside the carrier that say what it shows, why, and what follows (`reading()` in the rail or the note column; `composition.md` §4 "one carrier and its commentary"). The carrier keeps half the canvas; the readings take the column that remains at body size, never a caption. A page under 120 characters with no carrier is a claim page and its plan line says so; a page under 120 characters *with* a carrier is a chart with no reading — add the reading or move the chart to a page that has one. → receipt `chars`, `deck.rhythm.chars`
**Default — a text page is lifted by scale, not by a field**: a page whose carrier is prose or a statement (`carriers: statement`, `prose`, `quote`) earns its presence from the headline's scale (`display()` at cover size, 4-5 × the body), the strip under the deck line (`statBand()`, `ruledList()`, `columns()`), and the four corners (`kicker()` or a mark, `dateline()`, `source()`, the page number) — the measured mock text pages sit at 0.02-0.09 largest object and read as frontier. A card or a field under the text to raise its measured share is the weak default (§10); a table that is the carrier takes `tableRows(avail(top))` rows instead. → receipt `observe.presence`, `page_underfill`
**Default — edit the message before the box**: preserve the source's meaning, not its paragraph shape (the copy itself is condensed from the fact sheet in the source's own vocabulary — `SKILL.md` §2 step 2). Keep prose for nuance; turn a supported relationship into a diagram or comparison when that reduces explanation. Remove duplicate title/body wording and move implementation detail to notes before resizing. A balanced slide should not require a long paragraph to explain what its main visual means.
**Hard rule — when the subject is a face, a size, a color, or a material, draw it**: a type specimen, a size ramp, a swatch is content, not decoration; its value comes from the source. → manual (kit `specimen()`, swatches through `field()`)
**Hard rule — reading mode on the page**: presentation puts one claim and one dominant visual per slide; balanced puts the primary claim and its evidence on the same slide; text makes the slide stand alone. A balanced slide carrying a claim with no evidence beside it, or evidence with no claim, is under-authored. → manual
**Hard rule — structural devices encode**: numbering, kickers, dividers, labels, and marks appear only when they say something true — a number when order carries information, a kicker when it names a real section, a mark when the deck has sections. A device repeated on every slide by habit is decoration. → manual
**Hard rule — one paragraph, one text box**: a paragraph or list is one `addText` with `breakLine` between items, never a stack of single-line boxes. → runtime `text_fragmentation`
**Hard rule — a chart is native**: a bar or column drawn from rectangles is a picture of a chart; `addChart` / the kit `chart()` keeps the data editable. → runtime `dead_vector_chart`
**Default — the headline's contrast phrase**: 11 of the 26 measured mock pages set one phrase of the headline apart — the second line in the serif italic ("Intelligence, / at the edge."), a noun in the accent in the Korean and Chinese ones — and a16z colours the load-bearing noun of a chapter claim. The kit draws it as one accent run (`emph:` on `display()`, `poster()`, `title()`, `head()`; `runsWith()` keeps it whole across the eojeol wrap). It belongs to the cover, a claim page, or the one title that states the thesis — the phrase is the finding's noun or figure, never a connective — and not to every content title: repeated on each page it is a template, not a device (§8 structural devices). A title with the contrast phrase and a body with the marker highlight is two emphases on one page; choose one.
**Default — inline emphasis**: one load-bearing figure or noun per sentence lifted as a bold run in the accent or the regular weight; never connectives, every noun, or structural text (footer, axis, page number). The marker highlight (`emphasis()` with `{ mark: true }`, the a16z device — a phrase on the accent fill in the ink) is the strongest form and is used once per page, on a claim page or the takeaway beside a chart.

So the third callout on a deck has the anatomy of the first, and a state reads the same in a badge, a verdict cell, and a callout. A deck that needs another anatomy redefines the entry once before the slides; a per-call override of a size or a field is the tell. Each helper signs its shape, and the receipt reads the signatures back (`receipt.slides[].specs`, `deck.specs`: count, slides, variants, anatomies) — one anatomy per carrier is the spec kept; a second is the override to explain.

An icon marks a recognizable concept the copy names (`direction.md` §4); a row of icons over abstract nouns is decoration.

## 10. Common weak defaults — review, not automatic rejection
These are prompts to inspect the rendered result, not a style blacklist. A supplied brand or a purposeful composition can use grids, centered type, gradients, underlines, or restrained decoration. Factual fidelity, readable contrast, and non-clipping remain mandatory regardless of style.
- Numbering cards or sections "01 / 02 / 03" when the content is not an ordered sequence.
- An equal card grid on every content slide, or any card grid without a page job; cards inside cards; framed panels carrying no information; the same three-column module on consecutive slides.
- Accent lines under titles, header/footer bars, sidebar stripes, single-side card borders (`decorative_stripe`). Separate with air, a background tint, or a hairline between rows.
- Text-only pseudo-diagrams: a pipeline, data flow, or benchmark comparison as plain text cards instead of native charts, nodes, and connectors (the §8 Hard rules: one paragraph, one box; a chart is native).
- Purple-blue SaaS gradients as the default answer; cream/beige defaults (F5F5DC, FAF0E6, FAEBD7, FFF8E1); a new color per slide.
- The three default looks chosen without a reason from the subject: warm cream + high-contrast serif + terracotta; near-black + one acid accent; broadsheet hairlines + zero radius + dense columns.
- One face for every Korean deck regardless of style; body copy in a single weight; a document-density body under a presentation-scale title.
- A picture under title text without a scrim; a uniform overlay or black plate instead of a directional scrim; a picture that is merely decorative.
- Centered body paragraphs; low-contrast text; one styled slide beside plain ones; shadows on every container; page chrome drawn on slides instead of the master.

## 11. Validation before drawing
- Coverage: every relationship in the content is visible; none invented.
- Reading path: entry, progression, and endpoint are unambiguous.
- Removal: with color, icons, and garnish stripped, placement alone still communicates.
- Dominance: the most prominent element is what the slide is about.
- Visual: every content slide carries a chart, picture, shape construction, drawn specimen, or hero number (advisory `meaningful_visual_missing`).
