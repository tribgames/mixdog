# Composition

Owns how one slide is composed once the direction exists (`direction.md`). This is vocabulary the author composes with, not a catalog the slide picks from: there is no slide type to select, only a page job, a relationship, and a composition move the author decides before writing a coordinate. `kit.md` draws it; `pictures.md` adds the picture families.

Rule strength as in `direction.md`: **Hard rule** (with `→ runtime \`code\`` or `→ manual`), **Default**, **Reference**.

## 0. The page brief — decided before geometry (one plan line per slide)
Each slide's plan line in the brief (`SKILL.md` §3) names, in this order:
- **job** — cover · section · claim · evidence · process · comparison · structure · picture · closing (what the page does for the reader).
- **relationship** — the atom the content carries (§1), or `none` for a focal claim.
- **move** — what the reader now believes, decides, or does. **Hard rule — every slide states its move**: a slide that cannot state it is an outline defect — cut it or merge it, never decorate around it. → manual
- **composition** — the page-scale move (§2 lens + §3 recipe in ordinary words: "one dark field on the left third holding the number; evidence hangs from a vertical rule on the right").
- **carriers** — what carries the content: chart · table · picture · hero · statement · quote · specimen · diagram · prose · list (the runtime reads this back as information: `plan_promise_missing` is advisory).
- **texture** — prose · list · specimen · keywords (§8).
- **rhythm** — anchor · dense · breathing (§7).

## 1. Relationship atoms (Reference — vocabulary for the plan line)
| Atom | Meaning | Encode with | Topology axes to vary |
|---|---|---|---|
| order | sequence, progression, rank | position, numbering, direction, shared path | open or closed path; straight, bent, stepped, switchback, or coiled; level, rising, or falling; where the turns and the endpoint sit |
| link | dependency, exchange, influence | proximity when unmistakable, otherwise an edge | direct, hub, chain, split, merge, exchange, feedback; the fewest edges that carry the meaning |
| parent | one unit governs or decomposes into children | branching, indentation, nesting, scale | branch, indent, nest, radiate, scale |
| membership | units belong to a group, stage, lane | containment, shared field, band, repetition | enclose, band, lane, cluster, repeat, nest |
| contrast | peers, states, options compare | shared baseline, opposing regions, parallel framing | semantic axes, opposing or parallel fields, counterweight, before/after boundary |
| overlap | units share a subset or duty | intersecting regions with a legible common area | paired, chained, layered intersections |
| focal claim | one statement or number | scale and air | statement alone, hero number, pull quote, receded picture behind oversized type |
| evidence | a chart, table, or picture proves the claim | the evidence as the field | chart as spine with a takeaway, table with a verdict column, picture with annotation |

**Hard rule — counts never imply form**: a count of items never implies equal size, spacing, angle, or symmetry; closure, mirroring, centrality, and interlock require a meaning already in the content. → manual
**Default — one topology per relationship**: the same atom on two slides uses two different topology axes.

## 2. Composition lenses — the page-scale move
Before coordinates, name the move the page makes and the jobs it serves (surface, boundary, focal mark, shared region, counterweight, direction). Then compose from these lenses; none is a checklist, each is a way a page can be organized.
| Lens | Use when it strengthens the page |
|---|---|
| Page field | one large surface, outline, aperture, or off-canvas contour organizes the major zones instead of a card per unit — a dark third, a tinted band, a plane cut on a diagonal |
| Outline carrier | a frame, arc, bracket, or band with no fill and a coherent stroke gives bare text ownership without a heavy card |
| Nested fields | an inset contour, secondary surface, badge, or focal shape inside or across a larger field creates hierarchy; siblings, never cards inside cards |
| Continuity | independent shapes aligned or overlapped across zones reinforce the reading path; a rule the content hangs from; a figure crossing a column edge |
| Depth and contrast | filled, outlined, offset, and negative-space atoms combine; one lifted object above flat peers; one light source |
| Deck language | a corner, arc, slant, notch, or layering logic recurs across the deck with page-fit variation, never a cloned composition |

**Default — field before card**: compare a page field, an outline carrier, a nested field, or cross-slide continuity before stacked cards or uniform equal columns; the first workable arrangement's readability does not close this comparison. Cards are for peer modules with a page job; an equal card grid without that job is the failure this section exists to prevent (advisory `card_grid_overuse`).
**Default — text belongs to something**: every claim, evidence group, or peer sits on a structural carrier — a field, an outline, a rule it registers to, or a grid plane with visible micro-geometry. Text floating in empty space with no anchor reads un-designed.

## 3. Page recipes — back to front, omit every layer without a job (Reference)
| Page job | Back-to-front stack | Stop when |
|---|---|---|
| cover | hero field (dark, gradient, or picture) → optional scrim or wash → one purposeful contour or motif at full size → kicker + title + one line | copy is safe and title and field read as one object |
| section | quiet field or picture band → restrained wash → the motif at half size → numeral + title | the deck language is recognizable |
| claim | quiet field → the statement or number at scale → one emphasis run or attribution → air where it gives the focal element room (below or beside, entering high) | 40-60% of the canvas is deliberately empty |
| evidence | context field → local contrast (a plane under the chart, a stat band) → native chart/table/leaders at the largest scale → optional takeaway | the claim is native and the evidence is the largest element |
| process | context field → native relation lines (spine first) → nodes and labels → one state or direction focus | every connector is semantic and ends on a node edge |
| comparison | matched planes seamed by weight → optional shared wash or divider → matched labels → one difference marker on one side only | crop, elevation, and paint stay symmetric unless asymmetry is the claim |
| structure | one field or outline → hierarchy by nesting, indentation, or scale → labels registered to their unit | placement alone communicates with color stripped |
| picture | the picture family from `pictures.md` §2 → modifier → native type on the calm zone | text sits on a scrim or beside the picture, never on it bare |
| closing | receded field → echoed contour or gradient from the cover → the ask in the accent → contact/meta | the ask is the most legible line |

## 4. Starting structures — relationship → geometry (Reference, first placements only)
Canvas 13.33 × 7.5 in, margin `M` 0.6; safe area x 0.6-12.73, y 0.6-6.9. Proportion follows information weight; these are first placements, not slots.
| Content relationship | Useful starting structure | Starting geometry |
|---|---|---|
| one focal claim | single column with air, or a full-bleed field with floating text | column 8-10 in wide entering at y ≈ 1.6-2.4; 40-60% of the canvas empty |
| one number and its meaning | hero numeral + measured explanation beside it | numeral 72-96 pt at (M, 2.2) w 5; explanation w 5.5 at x 7.2 |
| equal comparison | symmetric split, or a true 2×2 with the seam at the semantic threshold | 1:1 with a 0.3-0.45 in gap; quadrants ≈ 5.8 × 2.3 |
| dominant evidence + takeaway | asymmetric split, heavy side holds the chart | 3:7 or 2:8 — chart 8-8.4 in wide, note column 3.3 |
| several numbers with one cause | stat band on one baseline, one hairline under | 3-5 numerals 54-60 pt on y ≈ 2.4, widths from `spans()` |
| options judged on criteria | native table with a verdict column | `addTable` at box(M, 1.8, W − 2M, 4.4), header in the accent, alternate rows paperAlt |
| same shape across groups | small multiples on one row, shared axis | `smallMultiples()` at box(M, 2.4, W − 2M, 3.8), 3-4 panels |
| level order | chevron run, or a rule with nodes at content-driven x | `chevrons()` at box(M, 2.0, W − 2M, 1.15); details under each stage |
| rising or falling order | stepped blocks on a diagonal | block i at (M + i·2.45, 5.3 − i·0.85), w 2.2 h 1.3, connectors edge to edge |
| a long order that wraps | switchback rows with a turn | rows on y 2.2 / 3.9 / 5.6, `S.rightArrow` / `S.leftArrow` runs, `S.downArrow` turns |
| a loop | block-arc segments around a center | `arc()` segments r 1.85-2.2, thickness 0.28, 6° gaps, labels outside at mid-angle |
| one center, many satellites | hub and spokes at content-driven angles | hub d 1.9 at (W/2 + 0.2, 4.5); satellites d 1.2 on r 2.2; `connector()` edge to edge |
| many into one / one into many | sources in a column, one target vertically centered | sources box(M, y, 2.8, 0.9) gap 0.3; target at x 8.5 |
| stages that feed back | a run plus one dashed return connector | return along y 1.5 (up, across, down) from the last stage to the first |
| a root with branches | indented tree with braces, or radiating rings | root at (M, 1.9) w 4; children indented 0.5 per level; `brace()` spanning each parent's children |
| who does what, when | swimlanes sharing seams | 2-4 lane fields, laneH = 4.8 / n from y 1.8; units as `S.roundRect` with 0.15 margin |
| named groups of items | brace groups in one column | 2-3 groups, each spanned by a `brace()` at the left, named at the brace tip; no boxes |
| levels of one hierarchy | trapezoid tiers on one taper | 3-4 tiers, widths monotonic, labels inside |
| shared and separate meaning | overlapping translucent ellipses | 2-3 ellipses d 3.0 overlapping 1.0-1.2, `transparency: 65`, shared meaning in the common region |
| items on two axes | one field with two rules crossing at the threshold | `field()` box(M, 1.8, W − 2M, 4.8); axis labels at the ends; items by their values |
| someone else's words | oversized quotation mark + quote + attribution | mark 120 pt at (M − 0.1, 1.3); quote 28-30 pt at (M + 0.9, 2.6) w 10-11 |
| the subject itself (type, color, spacing) | specimen rows, labels in the left 1.6 in | `specimen()` rows at box(M, top, 7.6-8, …); the claim as prose beside |

More peers than a structure holds is two slides or a different atom — six options are a table, not six cards. Content over the reading mode's budget (balanced ≈ 18 pt body; presentation holds about 30% less, text about 30% more) is split or cut, never squeezed with a smaller size than the scale gives.

## 5. Construction order and registration
**Default — spine → nodes → connectors → labels → garnish**: the spine fixes entry and direction before any node is placed; nodes get one home each; only unresolved semantic links become connectors; labels attach visibly to what they explain; garnish is whatever can be removed without losing meaning.
**Hard rule — registration**: connectors end on node boundaries, chevron tips enter the next notch, seams meet across the full width, nested shapes keep a visible margin, one light source per slide. → runtime `shape_overlap` for text; connectors and seams → manual

## 6. Weight, spacing, grid
**Hard rule — edges and gaps**: 0.5 in minimum from every slide edge (page number and source line may sit at 0.4 in; a deliberate bleed — a picture, a band — is exempt); blocks at least 0.3 in apart, except a numeral directly above its own label (0.05-0.1 in) and a kicker above its title (0.1 in). → runtime `edge_margin`, `shape_out_of_bounds`, `text_spacing_tight`, `shape_overlap`
**Hard rule — two spacing steps, not one**: every gap is either *within* (a thing and what belongs to it — `GAP.within` ≈ 0.12 in) or *between* (peers and blocks — `GAP.between` ≈ 0.45 in). One constant for both is the machine tell the eye reads as "nothing belongs to anything". → manual (kit `GAP`)
**Hard rule — peers are sized by weight**: a row of peers takes its widths from what each peer carries — measured content or a named `weight` — through `spans()` and `splitAt()`; a seam sits at 0.38-0.62 of the width, never in the middle by default. Equal division is a decision, made only when equality is the message, and the plan line says so. Inside a region, blocks stack by measure (`flow()`), not by a fixed slot each. → manual (kit `spans`, `splitAt`, `flow`; the deck-level tell → runtime `repetitive_composition`)
**Default — one grid**: peers share the same x for a column and the same baseline for a row; equal gaps in a row within 5%. The title is part of the composition, not a fixed header band: it moves with the page's role (head for most content, low band when a carrier owns the page, inside the field on a claim).
**Default — fill the frame with intent**: content spans the safe area; residual blank in one corner is a defect, while air around a focal element is design; breathing slides keep 40-60% empty on purpose. A takeaway band, decision rail, or call-to-action closes a page near the lower safe margin (bottom edge ≈ 6.8-6.95 in), and the field above it grows to meet it — a footer floating mid-canvas with a hollow beneath is the tell.
**Default — proportions follow information weight**: on a breathing slide the empty area sits where it gives the focal element room to be read, not as equal bands above and below a centered strip; on a dense slide the stat or stage the slide is about is the widest, and a comparison's changed side gets the larger plane.

## 7. Rhythm across the deck
**Default — roles alternate**: anchor (cover, section, closing: one statement, air, the motif at full size), dense (evidence, diagrams, comparisons), breathing (one hero number or quote, 40-60% empty). Alternate dense and breathing; open and close with anchors; usually at least one content slide changes the background field (a dark breathing slide in a light deck, or the reverse).
**Reference — reading the receipt's `observe`**: `air` in sequence is the density line (a healthy deck saws between dense ≈ 0.3-0.5 and breathing ≈ 0.6-0.8, and never flatlines); `quadrantAir` shows where the air sits (air beside the focal element is design, a lone empty corner is leftover); `largestShare` says whether the page has a dominant object; `textColumns.stray` counts boxes that share no left edge; `fills` shows the accent's real area against the 60/30/10 intent; `largestTextTop` across the deck shows whether the biggest type (title or hero) ever leaves the head; `centroid` is where the page's visual weight sits ([0.5, 0.5] is dead center) and `centroidOffset` how far it is from center against a tolerance that forgives vertical drift three times more than sideways drift (about 1 is at the edge of what reads as balanced; a focal element placed off center on purpose reads higher, and the plan line says why); `renderAir` (after a render) reads the pixels rather than the shape boxes, so a picture with an empty sky or a soft gradient field shows its real air — a slide with low `air` and high `renderAir` is carrying more emptiness than its shapes admit. Each is a number to weigh against the plan line, never a target.
**Default — variety, bounded**: the same composition move appears at most twice per deck and rarely on adjacent slides; variety comes from the topology axis and the carrier inside a repeated move, not from a new visual language on every slide. A closing device (takeaway band, callout, source line) repeats only where the slide has a claim to close. The runtime reports monotony readings as information (`repeated_layout_grammar`, `repeated_render_composition`, `flat_visual_rhythm`, `visual_role_variety_low`, `card_grid_overuse`, `under_composed_slide`, `slide_visual_density_low`); a deliberate composition overrides all of them.

## 8. Text on the page
**Hard rule — authored texture**: prose stays prose where continuity carries cause, argument, narrative, qualification, or emphasis; bullets or keywords only for genuinely parallel or ordered material — never because a list is easier to lay out. When text does not fit, widen the zone, reflow, or drop a block before converting prose to a list or shrinking the type. → manual
**Hard rule — when the subject is a face, a size, a color, or a material, draw it**: a type specimen, a size ramp, a swatch is content, not decoration; its value comes from the source. → manual (kit `specimen()`, swatches through `field()`)
**Hard rule — reading mode on the page**: presentation puts one claim and one dominant visual per slide; balanced puts the primary claim and its evidence on the same slide; text makes the slide stand alone. A balanced slide carrying a claim with no evidence beside it, or evidence with no claim, is under-authored. → manual
**Hard rule — structural devices encode**: numbering, kickers, dividers, labels, and marks appear only when they say something true — a number when order carries information, a kicker when it names a real section, a mark when the deck has sections. A device repeated on every slide by habit is decoration. → manual
**Hard rule — one paragraph, one text box**: a paragraph or list is one `addText` with `breakLine` between items, never a stack of single-line boxes. → runtime `text_fragmentation`
**Hard rule — a chart is native**: a bar or column drawn from rectangles is a picture of a chart; `addChart` / the kit `chart()` keeps the data editable. → runtime `dead_vector_chart`
**Default — inline emphasis**: one load-bearing figure or noun per sentence lifted as a bold run in the accent or the regular weight; never connectives, every noun, or structural text (footer, axis, page number).
**Default — copy is rewritten, never reinvented**: slide copy is condensed from the source in the deck's language with the source's own vocabulary; entity names, product names, figures, and abbreviations stay in their original form; a figure the source does not contain is not on the slide. → runtime `number_without_fact`

**Chart and table forms (Reference — each row names the relationship the form encodes, nothing more; not a ranking, and a deck with zero charts is valid when nothing on it is a quantity)**
| Encoded relationship | Form | Kit |
|---|---|---|
| change along a continuous order (time, stages) | line | `chart({ type: 'line' })` |
| magnitude across that order, or layered composition over it | area | `chart({ type: 'area' })` |
| category values from one baseline; one category the title names | column or bar; the accent on that one; a leader to a short note when the reason belongs on the chart | `chart({ type: 'col' \| 'bar', accent, note: { at, text } })` |
| several series over shared categories | grouped bars | `chart()` with 2-3 series |
| totals split into parts | stacked bars, labels inside | `addChart` with `barGrouping: 'stacked'` |
| the same shape across groups | small multiples on one axis | `smallMultiples()` |
| a start walked to an end by contributions | waterfall | `waterfall()` |
| two values per item and the gap between them | dumbbell | `dumbbell()` |
| an actual against its target or track on one scale | bullet | `chart({ overlap: true })` |
| parts of one whole (up to five) | doughnut | `chart({ type: 'doughnut' })` |
| several measures of one or two entities | radar | `chart({ type: 'radar' })` |
| two numeric variables per item | scatter | `chart({ type: 'scatter' })` |
| two variables and a third by size | bubble | `chart({ type: 'bubble' })` |
| one bounded proportion | gauge or share arc | `gauge()`, `arc()` |
| flat records, one stable field per column | record table | `table()` |
| entities × metrics, with a value, change, or status per cell | metric table | `table()`, status as a `badge()` word never color alone |
| criteria × alternatives with a decision | comparison matrix | `table({ verdict })` |
| capabilities × offerings as supported / partial / no | feature matrix | `table()` with ✓ ◐ – glyphs and a legend |
| grouped rows with subtotals | hierarchical table | `table()`, first column indented per level, subtotal rows bold on the tint |

**Hard rule — a rule is not a title underline**: a spanner or hairline directly under a title is the title-rule tell; in data-journalism the spanner drops *into* a stat band or a chart (above the band, below the numbers), and on anchors it frames the page at full width. → runtime `decorative_stripe`
**Hard rule — depth through restraint**: at most 2-3 floating objects per slide; resting shadow opacity 0.06-0.10; one weight tool per container (shadow, hairline, tint, or fill, never stacked); text glow and outline on one display element per deck, never body copy. → runtime `decorative_stripe` for stripes and title rules; shadows and stacking → manual

## 10. Machine tells — forbidden
- Numbering cards or sections "01 / 02 / 03" when the content is not an ordered sequence.
- An equal card grid on every content slide, or any card grid without a page job; cards inside cards; framed panels carrying no information; the same three-column module on consecutive slides.
- Accent lines under titles, header/footer bars, sidebar stripes, single-side card borders (`decorative_stripe`). Separate with air, a background tint, or a hairline between rows.
- Text-only pseudo-diagrams: a pipeline, data flow, or benchmark comparison as plain text cards instead of native charts, nodes, and connectors.
- A paragraph as a stack of single-line boxes (`text_fragmentation`); a chart built from rectangles (`dead_vector_chart`).
- Purple-blue SaaS gradients as the default answer; cream/beige defaults (F5F5DC, FAF0E6, FAEBD7, FFF8E1); a new color per slide.
- The three default looks chosen without a reason from the subject: warm cream + high-contrast serif + terracotta; near-black + one acid accent; broadsheet hairlines + zero radius + dense columns.
- One face for every Korean deck regardless of style; body copy in a single weight; a document-density body under a presentation-scale title.
- A picture under title text without a scrim; a uniform overlay or black plate instead of a directional scrim; a picture that is merely decorative.
- Centered body paragraphs; low-contrast text; one styled slide beside plain ones; shadows on every container; page chrome drawn on slides instead of the master.

## 11. Validation before drawing
- Coverage: every relationship in the content is visible; none invented.
- Reading path: entry, progression, and endpoint are unambiguous.
- Removal: with color, icons, and garnish stripped, placement alone still communicates.
- Dominance: the most prominent element is what the slide is about.
- Visual: every content slide carries a chart, picture, shape construction, drawn specimen, or hero number (advisory `meaningful_visual_missing`).
